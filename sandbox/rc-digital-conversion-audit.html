Verdict: the site sells the brand well, but it hides the ways to buy. The positioning is distinctive and the build quality is high. What's missing is everything a service-business owner needs to say yes: a phone number, proof with numbers, a human, a price signal, and a fast low-commitment way to start. Right now there is exactly one conversion path — a four-step assessment form — and no way to measure who falls out of it.

Critical — blocking sales

Critical There is no way to contact you

No phone number, no email address, no tel: or mailto: link anywhere on the site — not in the header, footer, or contact section. The site literally says "Direct contact. Zero bloat." and offers zero direct contact. Trades owners buy by talking to a person; many will not fill out a form at all.

Put a real phone number (tap-to-call) and email in the header and footer of every page, and a "prefer to talk? call/text us" line beside the form.

Critical One conversion path, and it's heavy

The only way to become a lead is the 4-step "Trade growth assessment." Step 1 alone asks for industry (54-option dropdown), business name, your name, email, phone, follow-up preference, and website — 7 fields before the first Continue. Every extra field costs real completion rate; four gated steps compounds it.

Offer three lanes: call/text now · book a 15-minute call · a 60-second short form (industry, name, phone — enrich the rest on the follow-up call). Keep the full assessment as an optional deep path, not the toll gate.

Critical Proof without numbers isn't proof

The Results library contains one field report — Frame Restoration, your own venture — plus a "reserved for the next operator" placeholder. No client testimonials, no review scores, no metrics (leads per month, ranking wins, revenue impact), no before/after. The copy promises "verified" and "evidence" but shows none a buyer can check.

Publish Frame's real numbers (calls, form fills, rankings by territory, time-to-launch), add 2–3 named quotes with faces, and show actual screenshots of the work instead of abstract diagrams.

High — costing you traffic and trust

High Canonical tag bug is nuking your SEO

Sub-pages declare rel="canonical" → https://rcdigitalsystems.com (verified on /work). That tells Google every page is a duplicate of the homepage — so your 54 industry pages and the results library are likely being dropped from the index entirely.

Set each page's canonical to its own URL. Then verify indexing in Search Console. This is a one-line-per-page fix with outsized payoff.

High No structured data

No JSON-LD anywhere — no Organization, LocalBusiness, Service, or FAQ schema. For a firm selling local-search systems, the site should demonstrate the craft it sells.

Add Organization + Service schema sitewide and FAQ schema on industry pages.

High The voice may fly over your buyer's head

"Doctrine," "after-action loop," "deployment phases," "command layer" — the military-ops framing is distinctive, but a plumber with one truck searching "get more plumbing calls" may read it as consultant-speak. You told me the audience is trades and hard workers; the current copy is written for multi-market empire builders. Both can't lead.

Decide the primary buyer. If it's working owner-operators: plain-talk headline and CTAs up top (the sandbox redesign's voice), keep the systems/command depth one level down for the ambitious minority. If it's genuinely multi-market operators, keep the voice — but then the proof bar is even higher.

High Nobody home

No founder name, no face, no story, no photos of real people or real job sites. Small-business owners hire people they trust, and trust is personal. An anonymous "system" asks them to buy from a logo.

Add a short "who you're working with" section: your name, your photo, one paragraph on why an operator built this for operators. It reinforces the positioning instead of fighting it.

Medium — friction and blind spots

Medium No price signal

Zero indication of cost or engagement model. Owners self-disqualify silently when they can't tell if this is $500 or $50,000.

Publish a starting-at range or the engagement shapes (project build / monthly system). "Fixed price, in writing, before we start" is itself a selling point.

Medium No response-time promise or risk reversal

Nothing tells a submitter what happens next or when. Speed-to-lead is the #1 close-rate lever in the trades — sell it by practicing it.

"You'll hear from us within one business day" next to every CTA, and an instant confirmation text/email on submit.

Medium You can't measure the funnel

Vercel Web Analytics is not enabled on the project (API returns not-found). There's a custom analytics beacon in the code, but wherever it reports, there's no visible step-level funnel for the 4-step form — so you can't see where people quit.

Enable analytics (Vercel WA or PostHog — your CRM already uses PostHog), fire events per form step, and review drop-off weekly.

Medium Leads should land in your CRM with attribution

You built lead management and a multi-touch attribution engine into the RC Digital CRM. The site form should feed it directly — source, industry, and UTM intact — instead of living as a separate pipeline.

Point the form at a Supabase edge function that inserts into the CRM leads table with attribution fields. Every follow-up then runs through the pipeline you already track.

Medium Texting consent

The form offers "Text" as a follow-up preference with no SMS consent language. If you text leads, you want express consent on record (TCPA).

One checkbox line: "OK to text me about my request." Cheap now, expensive later.
